A change is a scene task written the way you wrote one in your commented-out cinderella-beautified scene (`cindy.46:94-105`): `(change (TYPE WHO OLD) (TYPE WHO NEW))`, on its own or inside `(causes CAUSER ...)`. Stage 3 does three things with it, and stage 6 a fourth:

1. **Who takes part.** Your `process-task-description` makes a task's second word the one it is for, and asks that one to note its part in the scene (`scene.9:458-465`, where you asked "what about other participents?"). A change on its own would have the actor `(personality cinderella shy)` note it, and every job that loads the film description stopped with NO-SUCH-ACTOR. Now a change on its own is for the changed character, and every character a change changes notes its part. A relationship change changes both of its characters; a relationship the story never gave is made first, as CINDY's define makes one.

   **What counts as a change.** A change is made only by a convey or establish task (the scene's tasks, `scene.9:11-13`) whose own description is the change, or which has it inside `(causes x ...)`. It must change a lasting description: physical, personality, movement, role-in-story or relationship, the descriptions "assumed to be permanent" (L4394-4396), not an emotional state. A change anywhere else is wanted, prevented or undone, not made: inside `(wants x ...)`, `(prevents x ...)` or `(undoes x (from-scene ...))`, or in a display task. Ani leaves it alone, and the task notes why: `(revival-repair: what change-not-made was (change ...) now left-alone because (inside wants))`. `storyOutline` takes the same changes, so no fresh look is announced for one that isn't made, and the film page's story check says so for such a task. A change that names no character of the story stops with that said ("CINDRELLA IS NOT A CHARACTER OF THE STORY"), where your code would stop on the missing actor with nothing said about the change.
2. **A fresh look** ("rederive the dynamics ... as if the character were brand new", p. 168, L4396-4402). This is an extra pass-1 job for each scene that changes someone. It runs DATEST's `init` with the change made after CINDY loads and before `study-descriptor-use`. The old words are forgotten, and the character is described with the new ones through your own `process initial description`, as her initial description (your decision 8 of 11 October). Then Ani makes her choices again, in her own order, with every choice that isn't the changed character's held as it was. Her new values go to `KEN1;<fn1> BECOME`, written the way `save-answers` writes `KEN;CP`. A second job, a full recompute with everyone chosen afresh, is kept only for comparison (your decision 1).
3. **Who they have become.** In every later scene, RUN's `to-do-before-running` (`run.65:48`) loads the earlier scenes' BECOME files and makes them so: the descriptions change, and the choice points take her new values. The scene's establish tasks then read the new words, and its special dynamics carry the new values. If MEND is loaded, it narrows any `(not X)` and reads any `none` as medium, as it does for the CP's values.

4. **Showing the change (stage 6).** In the scene of the change, Ani shows it: the cause goes to the one it changes, and once they touch she changes as she wanders about, or she wanders as she changes if nothing causes it; Ani bridges the gap between how she moved and looked and how she will, over a time she decides, gradually, or at once if the cause is magical (the revival's rule). With APPEAR, the fresh look chooses how she looks again too, and later scenes draw her as she has become. See "Showing the change (stage 6)", below; your cinderella-beautified scene now plays ("Your cinderella-beautified scene plays"), and the film page shows all this, and offers your scene in "Change the story" ("On the page (stage 6)").

## Decisions for Ken (stage 6)

1. **A magical cause changes her at once.** The thesis says a transition is occasionally instantaneous and more typically gradual (p. 166) and that an abrupt change could be misread (p. 169), but not which are which. The revival's rule: instantaneous when a cause is described as magical, otherwise gradual. In your cinderella-beautified the fairy godmother is magical, so Cinderella's clothes become clear at once. Say if her change should be gradual too (it would bridge over ten seconds an e-fold), or what else should make a change instantaneous.
2. **Gradual is your slowest speed**, ten seconds an e-fold (`disply.311:1104-1106`), so a change by 2 takes 6.9 seconds before the stretch. Say if gradual should be medium (five).
3. **All of a change's elements change together**, over the time the largest needs (the design's choice): a shape or hue counts as one e-fold.
4. **The cause goes to her; then she wanders while she changes, and the cause stands.** goes-to-and-transforms is modeled on move-to-and-hits (a walk to contact, then the act); careful of collisions, as goes-to-to-accompany's walk is, where your goes-to-to-hit goes regardless. With nothing to bridge, or an instantaneous change, she wanders for one walk across the stage, so that the change is seen; a gradual change is shown for as long as the bridges take.
5. **CHANGE requires MEND.** Its wander is MEND9's (placing and walks), the one changed is brought on by MEND6's bring-on, and the cause's walk is estimated by MEND5's; a change story is a new story, which needs MEND anyway. `check-1978 --extensions change` now loads MEND too, and is byte for byte as before.
6. **The bridge's Director message steps over your double growth** (finding 1). Your own change is left as it is, for SLIPS.
7. **The wander counts walks on half a walk across** (finding 4), more walks than MEND9's, each shorter, so that the change is shown for its whole time.
8. **A relationship change shows only its first character** (the one whose relationship it is) wandering; the second is chosen again, and its bridges, if any, would run with no way of showing beside them, while SCENE has her wander or extends the part before.
9. **The new self is still shown from the next scene on.** The scene of the change reads her description as it was when it began (the plan's choice; sequence-aware descriptions are deferred): in Change of Heart the scene's other tasks are planned from the evil stepmother's description, and she moves as she did until the bridges begin.
